African Savanna

Venture across vast tropical grasslands, home to legendary megafauna herds, apex predators, and dynamic seasonal cycles.

ECOSYSTEM OVERVIEW

The Realm of Endless Grasslands

The African savanna is a rolling grassland scattered with shrubs and isolated trees, lying between tropical rainforests and desert biomes. Characterized by a warm climate and distinct wet and dry seasons, it supports the highest concentration of large mammal species on Earth.

From seasonal wildebeest migrations to specialized acacia trees engineered to survive droughts, this biome is a masterclass in ecological resilience and predator-prey dynamics.

BIOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE

Megaherbivores & Predator Webs

For students studying wildlife conservation, the savanna provides a clear blueprint of trophic cascades. Large herbivores like elephants and zebras actively shape the landscape by grazing grass and knocking down trees, which prevents the habitat from turning into a thick forest.

In turn, apex predators like lions and cheetahs regulate herbivore populations, keeping the entire grassland ecosystem balanced and flourishing.
