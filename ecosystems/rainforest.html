ECOSYSTEM OVERVIEW

The Emerald Lungs of Our Planet

Tropical rainforests are Earth's oldest living ecosystems, with some surviving in their present form for over 70 million years. Spanning across equatorial regions, they maintain global climate balance by absorbing massive quantities of carbon dioxide.

From the high emergent layer down to the dark forest floor, every single square inch teems with complex food webs, exotic wildlife, and vital natural resources.

BIOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE

Sustaining Global Biodiversity & Life

For students and researchers, rainforests serve as the ultimate classroom for ecological symbiosis. Every species—from canopy-dwelling toucans to tiny microorganisms in the soil—plays an irreplaceable role in nutrient recycling.

Understanding these intricate dependencies helps conservationists combat deforestation, protect endangered wildlife habitats, and preserve the planet's most critical natural pharmacy.
